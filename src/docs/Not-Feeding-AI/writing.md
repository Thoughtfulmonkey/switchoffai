# Writing

Large language models (LLMs) are trained on huge amounts of text, mostly without the author's consent. Most companies, such as OpenAI, use the defence that they're using information that's [publicly available on the Internet](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7842364-how-chatgpt-and-our-foundation-models-are-developed). However, making your writing publicly available does not make it public domain, you still retain the copyright (see Giving away your rights). The source of training data for LLMs is a complex legal issue that's still being debated, but there are steps that you can take to protect your writing from being consumed by AI.

!!! note "Accessibility" 
    Any attempts to make your writing less readable by AI are likely to make your writing less accessible to humans. For example, you might prevent someone who uses a screen reader from being able to access your writing.

!!! note "Giving away your rights"
    When you use a website or app, you have to agree to their terms and conditions. You often need to give away some of your rights, including some aspects of copyright, when you agree to those terms and conditions.

## ShieldFont

[ShieldFont](https://shieldfont.org/) is a font that makes the text seen by a website scraper, such as those used to gather AI training data, look different to text seen by a human reader. An important part of the project is that the version of the text presented to the website scraper seems grammatically correct, but logically nonsensical. And so it contributes to poisoning any AI model that uses it.

## Ghost Font

[Ghost Font](https://www.mixfont.com/ghost-font) hides a message in an animated wall of noise.

## Decoy Font

[Decoy Font](https://www.mixfont.com/experiments/decoy-font) uses two overlapping fonts: one that is detected by AI, and one that is intended to be readable only by humans.
